# Changelog

Each version has two sections: Players, what a player or pack developer sees, and Consumers, what a mod building against Gearworks can use or will see change. A published version never changes: a fix is the next patch (`docs/agents/releases.md`).

## Unreleased

### Players

- The suits are now named **Iron Suit**, **Diamond Suit** and **Netherite Suit**, where they were Iron, Diamond and Netherite Gear; the Gear Grid, Gear Upgrades and Gear Bench keep their names. (#11)

### Consumers

- **Breaking:** the Java that means the Suit says Suit, leaving Gear for the family of the Suit and the Bore Lance (ADR 0005): `Gear` is now `Suit` (and `Gear.Totals` `Suit.Totals`), `GearItem` is `SuitItem`, `GearTicker` is `SuitTicker`, and `GearContent.IRON_GEAR`, `DIAMOND_GEAR` and `NETHERITE_GEAR` are `IRON_SUIT`, `DIAMOND_SUIT` and `NETHERITE_SUIT`. Item, data map, component and attribute modifier ids keep `gear`, and Gear Grid, Gear Upgrade and Gear Bench keep their names. (#10)

## 0.1.0

### Players

- **Iron Gear**, the tier-1 suit, worn in the chest slot with an iron chestplate's armor. Its 9×3 **Gear Grid**, whose columns stand over the inventory's, opens from the worn suit with a key (J by default), and holds the first three Gear Upgrades: **Battery** (1×2, 100,000 FE), **Solar** (1×1, 5 FE/t in daylight under open sky) and **Speed** (2×2, about +26% walking speed while it has FE, drawing 20 FE/t while walking). Any mod's charger fills the suit. Speed upgrades sum toward the `speedCeiling` server config (+100% by default) and never reach it. (#1)
- The worn suit's FE stays on the HUD, a bar and its numbers in the top-left corner, while the suit holds a battery. The `energyHud` client config hides it (`show`) or moves it to another corner (`corner`). The Config button in the mod list edits it, and the `speedCeiling` server config in single player. The suit's FE now reads with grouped digits in the client's language, on the HUD, its tooltip and its Gear Grid. (#5)
- The **Gear Bench**, a placed block that holds a suit. A click on an upgrade in its grid picks it, and Raise lifts its **Level** by one, with no cap. Each Level costs FE and `c:`-tagged materials, more by a constant factor (1.25 by default) each Level, from copper or iron and redstone up to netherite and nether stars from Level 15. Any mod's pipes, hoppers and cables stock the bench, which takes only what the next Level costs and gives nothing back; its screen shows the suit's grid at full size, each material's slot, and a bar of the FE stocked against the FE the Level costs, and Raise spends exactly that cost once all of it is stocked. A Level stays on the upgrade when it moves to another suit, and each Level adds to what it gives: +50,000 FE a battery, +2 FE/t a solar, +10% walking speed and +5 FE/t a speed upgrade, whose sum still never reaches the `speedCeiling`. (#2)
- Gearworks' items are in the **Works** creative tab the -works mods share, and in no vanilla tab. The Gear Bench's empty suit slot and material slots show faded what they take, each material at the count the next Level needs. Gearworks nests libworks-runtime, so nothing more is installed.
- **Diamond Gear** and **Netherite Gear**, tiers 2 and 3, with a diamond and a netherite chestplate's armor and Gear Grids with more rows (9×4 and 9×6); netherite gear, like the chestplate, survives fire and lava. Diamond gear is crafted from iron gear and eight diamonds, and netherite gear smithed from diamond gear with a netherite upgrade template and a netherite ingot; each keeps every upgrade where it sits, its Level, and the suit's FE. Three more Gear Upgrades: **Shield** (2×2) absorbs about 21% of each hit that armor leaves, spending 200 FE for each point it absorbs, and nothing once the suit is empty; several shields absorb more together and still spend each point at their mean price, not their sum; **Jump** (1×2) raises jump strength by about 13% while the suit has FE, each jump spending 50 FE; **Night Vision** (1×1, one per suit) gives night vision while it draws 10 FE/t, and leaves a potion's alone. A shield Level adds 5% absorbed, a jump Level 5% jump strength and 10 FE a jump; a night vision Level takes about 1 FE/t off its draw, less as it nears the `nightVisionFloor` server config (1 FE/t), which it never goes under. Shield and jump upgrades sum toward the `shieldCeiling` (80% absorbed) and `jumpCeiling` (+50% jump strength, under three blocks) server configs and never reach them. (#3)
- In the Gear Grid, a shift-click sends an upgrade to the inventory when it has room, and a click on an upgrade with the same upgrade at the same Level in hand adds it to that stack. (#3)
- The suits and upgrades have Gearworks' own art. Each tier has an icon of its own, Futureazoo's flight chestplate retinted to its material with an orange gem (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), and is worn in a look of its own, plates of its material around a glowing orange gem, where it used to look like a plain chestplate; the upgrade icons are drawn as one set. (#7)
- The suit's gem glows: worn, it is lit in the dark and pulses every two seconds, and the suits' icons pulse with it.
- **Flight** (2×5, one per suit), the capstone, fits only netherite gear's grid: creative-style flight while the suit has FE, drawing 200 FE/t in the air. Each Level takes about 20 FE/t off the draw, less as it nears the `flightFloor` server config (20 FE/t), which it never goes under, and never changes how fast the player flies. A suit that runs dry mid-air lets the player down slowly, at no cost and without fall damage. It is crafted from four netherite ingots, four feathers and a nether star, and its Levels cost netherite, then diamonds, then nether stars. (#4)
- The Gear Bench has art of its own, a plated casing with an orange core let into its work top, where it used to borrow the smithing table's top and iron block sides, and the suit it holds floats above it, turning, for every player nearby to see.

### Consumers

- An item with a `gearworks:gear_upgrade` item data map entry (`width`, `height`, `stat`, `amount`, optional `draw`) fits the Gear Grid with no code; `gearworks:gear_grid` sizes each suit's grid, at most 9 columns by 6 rows. The suit exposes `Capabilities.Energy.ITEM` over the sum of its batteries. (#1)
- A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry takes optional `amount_per_level` and `draw_per_level`, added once per Level. The `gearworks:level_cost` item data map prices an upgrade's Levels (`energy`, `growth`, and `stages` of up to four `{tag, count}` materials each from a Level `from` on); an upgrade without an entry is never raised. An upgrade's Level is the `gearworks:level` component. The Gear Bench exposes `Capabilities.Item.BLOCK` and `Capabilities.Energy.BLOCK` on every face. (#2)
- A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry's `stat` also takes `shield` (`amount` the fraction of a hit absorbed, `draw` the FE for each point absorbed), `jump` (`amount` the fraction of jump strength added, `draw` the FE a jump spends) and `night_vision` (`draw` FE per tick, which `draw_per_level` lowers toward the `nightVisionFloor` instead of raising; `amount` is not read; a suit holds one). `gearworks:gear_grid` sizes `gearworks:diamond_gear` and `gearworks:netherite_gear`, which expose `Capabilities.Energy.ITEM` as iron gear does. (#3)
- A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry's `stat` also takes `flight` (`draw` FE per tick in the air, which `draw_per_level` lowers toward the `flightFloor`; `amount` is not read; a suit holds one). The suit gives flight through NeoForge's `neoforge:creative_flight` attribute, with the modifier `gearworks:gear_flight`, and leaves any other source of flight alone. (#4)
