# Changelog

Each version has two sections: Players, what a player or pack developer sees, and Consumers, what a mod building against Gearworks can use or will see change. A published version never changes: a fix is the next patch (`docs/agents/releases.md`).

## Unreleased

### Players

- Four new **Generators**, Gear Upgrades that make FE for the Suit as solar does, each adding up with others of its kind, and none making FE or spending anything once the Suit is full. A Generator's fractions of an FE now add up too, so one making 2.5 FE a tick makes 5 every two (#18):
  - **Nuclear Upgrade** (3×3): 40 FE/t, +10 a Level, day or night, under a roof or not. It is crafted from a nether star, two netherite ingots, a redstone block and five iron ingots, and its Levels cost gold and diamonds, then netherite and nether stars from Level 5, and far more FE than solar's.
  - **Kinetic Upgrade** (1×2): 10 FE, +2 a Level, for each block walked or sprinted on foot, a jump's time in the air and wading through shallow water included; none while flying, gliding, swimming or riding. Crafted from a piston, copper and redstone.
  - **Combustion Upgrade** (2×2): burns fuel from your inventory, the main inventory before the hotbar, giving 2 FE, +0.5 a Level, for each tick of its furnace burn time, so a coal gives 3,200 FE over 80 seconds. It burns only the item tag `gearworks:suit_fuel`, coal, charcoal, coal blocks and blaze rods by default, and never planks or tools. The Suit keeps one fire, saved on it, which waits while the Suit is off or full; more combustion upgrades burn it faster. Crafted from a blast furnace, iron and redstone.
  - **Metabolic Upgrade** (1×2): 20 FE/t, +5 a Level, from your saturation, never your hunger, so it stops once you have none left and never starves you. A point of saturation is 1,000 FE, set by `metabolicEnergy` in the server config. Crafted from a golden carrot, copper and redstone.

- Gearworks nests libworks-runtime 0.1.12, where it nested 0.1.8.

### Consumers

- `Stat.NUCLEAR`, `KINETIC`, `COMBUSTION` and `METABOLIC`, `GearContent.NUCLEAR_UPGRADE`, `KINETIC_UPGRADE`, `COMBUSTION_UPGRADE` and `METABOLIC_UPGRADE`, the suit's `gearworks:combustion_fire` and `gearworks:spare_fe` components, the item tag `gearworks:suit_fuel` (`SuitGenerators.SUIT_FUEL`), `Suit.Totals.count` and `GearworksConfig.metabolicEnergy`. A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry names any of the four Stats to make its own Generator. (#18)

## 0.2.0

### Players

- Gearworks' description in the mod list names the Bore Lance beside the Suit.
- The suits are now named **Iron Suit**, **Diamond Suit** and **Netherite Suit**, where they were Iron, Diamond and Netherite Gear; the Gear Grid, Gear Upgrades and Gear Bench keep their names. (#11)
- The **Bore Lance**, a spear that is also a mining tool, in iron and diamond. The **Iron Bore Lance** is crafted from an iron spear ringed with copper and redstone, as the Iron Suit is, and the **Diamond Bore Lance** from the iron one and three diamonds; each keeps the enchantments it was made with. Each is its material's spear with a little more attack damage, a harder charged hit and twice its material's durability, with **Lunge** built in, I on iron and II on diamond, which no grindstone takes off; it lunges by vanilla's rules, from the jab, with food above 6, not riding, gliding or in water. Each mines rock up to its material's level at its material's speed: a left-click jabs, and holding it mines, so mining never lunges, the lance held out at the full reach of its jab until let go. A lance mining a block breaks its **Bore** with it: 1×1 on iron and up to 3×3 on diamond, on the face it hit and three blocks deep, skipping any block harder than the one hit or that it doesn't mine, each block dropping where it stood under the lance's Fortune or Silk Touch and costing 1 durability. A key (B by default) steps the lance in the main hand through its Bores, widest first; the lance keeps its choice, shows it in its tooltip and says it on the action bar when it changes. Holding left-click while sneaking mines a single block. The iron lance breaks; the diamond lance never does, stopping at its last durability, worn out, when it neither bores nor lunges until repaired. Pack authors set what a Bore breaks with the block tag `gearworks:mineable/bore_lance`, a pickaxe's by default. (#12)
- **Lunge Boring**: while a Bore Lance's Lunge carries the player forward, the lance breaks its Bore through what lies ahead of them, three deep from as far as they have come, up to a block per level of Lunge, straight on the way they faced, and always the two blocks they fill, so the iron lance's 1×1 Bore opens a way they fit through. A lunge breaks whatever the lance's tier mines, rock or not and whatever `gearworks:mineable/bore_lance` holds, and passes through air, water and grass. The dash stops at the first block of the Bore beyond the lance's tier, unbreakable or protected, and breaks nothing past it: obsidian stops the iron lance, and only bedrock and its like stop the diamond one. Each block costs 10 durability, set by `lungeBoringCost` in the server config, so a lance that wears out or breaks partway stops there. (#13)
- The **Netherite Bore Lance**, smithed from the diamond one with a netherite upgrade template and a netherite ingot, keeping its enchantments but Unbreaking and Mending, which it drops with its wear. It is the netherite spear with a little more attack damage and a harder charged hit, mines at netherite's level and speed in Bores up to 5×3×3, has **Lunge III** built in, survives fire and lava, and has no durability: it runs on FE instead, holding 1,000,000, which any mod's charger fills. A lunge costs 2,000 FE a level of Lunge and needs no food; each block of a Bore, the hit one included, costs 50 FE, and each block Lunge Boring breaks 500 FE, so a lance that runs dry partway stops there. Dry, it is still a full spear and mines single blocks at its speed, but neither lunges nor bores; its tooltip shows its FE and says when it is dry, or holds too little to lunge. A worn Suit charges it in either hand at 1,000 FE a tick, only from what the Suit holds over 25% of its batteries. Unbreaking and Mending can't be put on it, and do nothing on it. Each number is in the server config: `netheriteLanceCapacity`, `netheriteLanceLungeEnergy`, `netheriteLanceBoreEnergy`, `netheriteLanceLungeBoringEnergy`, `netheriteLanceChargeRate` and `netheriteLanceChargeReserve`. (#14)
- **Breaking:** the **Flight Upgrade** flies slower when fresh and faster as it levels: at Level 0 about half of creative's flying speed, with each Level adding to it, so it is back at creative's speed by Level 9 and approaches twice it without ever reaching it. Its Levels still lower its FE draw. Its tooltips show the speed, and the server config's `flightCeiling` sets how fast it can get. A player whose game mode or another mod lets them fly keeps their own flying speed. (ADR 0006)
- **Boost** (1×3, one per suit): a key, unbound until you bind it, throws a flying player along their gaze, as a firework throws a glider, for a 25-tick Thrust that carries them as fast whichever way they look. Each Boost spends 1,000 FE and starts a 5-second Cooldown, shown as a thin orange bar beside the HUD's energy bar; a press in the Cooldown, short of FE or not flying fizzles. Its Thrust pulls toward about 1.2 blocks a tick at Level 0, near a rocket's 1.5 by Level 3, bending toward the `boostCeiling` server config (2.5) and never reaching it. Each Level adds 0.2 to its pull before the bend, takes about 100 FE off its cost toward the `boostFloor` (200 FE) and about half a second off its Cooldown toward the `boostCooldownFloor` (25 ticks, and never shorter than the Thrust, `boostTicks`). Landing ends the Thrust. In creative mode a Boost is free but still cools down. It is crafted from four gold ingots, four firework rockets and a dispenser, and its Levels cost gold and gunpowder, then diamonds, then netherite and nether stars.

### Consumers

- `GearContent.NETHERITE_BORE_LANCE`, which exposes `Capabilities.Energy.ITEM` and stores its FE in the `gearworks:energy` component the Suit uses. (#14)
- **Breaking:** a flight upgrade's `amount` in the `gearworks:gear_upgrade` data map is now read, as its flying speed in fractions of creative's, and `amount_per_level` raises it toward the `flightCeiling`; an entry written with `amount: 1` flies at about 79% of creative's speed, and faster as it levels only if it sets `amount_per_level`. (ADR 0006)
- The Boost Stat, `Stat.BOOST`, `GearContent.BOOST_UPGRADE`, `Suit.Totals.cooldown` and `GearworksConfig.cooldownFloor`. A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry takes optional `cooldown` and `cooldown_per_level` fields, in ticks, which only Boost reads; every entry without them reads as before. `GearUpgrade` gains `cooldown` and `cooldownPerLevel` components, and its old seven-argument constructor stays, for an upgrade that never waits.
- `GearContent.IRON_BORE_LANCE` and `DIAMOND_BORE_LANCE`, the Bore Lance's `gearworks:bore` component, its recipe type `gearworks:crafting_bore_lance`, and the block tag `gearworks:mineable/bore_lance`. (#12)
- **Breaking:** the Java that means the Suit says Suit, leaving Gear for the family of the Suit and the Bore Lance (ADR 0005): `Gear` is now `Suit` (and `Gear.Totals` `Suit.Totals`), `GearItem` is `SuitItem`, `GearTicker` is `SuitTicker`, and `GearContent.IRON_GEAR`, `DIAMOND_GEAR` and `NETHERITE_GEAR` are `IRON_SUIT`, `DIAMOND_SUIT` and `NETHERITE_SUIT`. Item, data map, component and attribute modifier ids keep `gear`, and Gear Grid, Gear Upgrade and Gear Bench keep their names. (#10)
- Gearworks' game-test code and structures no longer ship in its jar (libworks ADR 0011): a Consumer's game tests and dev client no longer register Gearworks' test types, `gearworks:code` and `gearworks:day_time`, nor carry its `gametest/platform` structure. Gearworks builds on libworks-plugin 0.4.0.

## 0.1.0

### Players

- **Iron Gear**, the tier-1 suit, worn in the chest slot with an iron chestplate's armor. Its 9×3 **Gear Grid**, whose columns stand over the inventory's, opens from the worn suit with a key (J by default), and holds the first three Gear Upgrades: **Battery** (1×2, 100,000 FE), **Solar** (1×1, 5 FE/t in daylight under open sky) and **Speed** (2×2, about +26% walking speed while it has FE, drawing 20 FE/t while walking). Any mod's charger fills the suit. Speed upgrades sum toward the `speedCeiling` server config (+100% by default) and never reach it. (#1)
- The worn suit's FE stays on the HUD, a bar and its numbers in the top-left corner, while the suit holds a battery. The `energyHud` client config hides it (`show`) or moves it to another corner (`corner`). The Config button in the mod list edits it, and the `speedCeiling` server config in single player. The suit's FE now reads with grouped digits in the client's language, on the HUD, its tooltip and its Gear Grid. (#5)
- The **Gear Bench**, a placed block that holds a suit. A click on an upgrade in its grid picks it, and Raise lifts its **Level** by one, with no cap. Each Level costs FE and `c:`-tagged materials, more by a constant factor (1.25 by default) each Level, from copper or iron and redstone up to netherite and nether stars from Level 15. Any mod's pipes, hoppers and cables stock the bench, which takes only what the next Level costs and gives nothing back; its screen shows the suit's grid at full size, each material's slot, and a bar of the FE stocked against the FE the Level costs, and Raise spends exactly that cost once all of it is stocked. A Level stays on the upgrade when it moves to another suit, and each Level adds to what it gives: +50,000 FE a battery, +2 FE/t a solar, +10% walking speed and +5 FE/t a speed upgrade, whose sum still never reaches the `speedCeiling`. (#2)
- Gearworks' items are in the **Works** creative tab the -works mods share, and in no vanilla tab. The Gear Bench's empty suit slot and material slots show faded what they take, each material at the count the next Level needs. Gearworks nests libworks-runtime, so nothing more is installed.
- **Diamond Gear** and **Netherite Gear**, tiers 2 and 3, with a diamond and a netherite chestplate's armor and Gear Grids with more rows (9×4 and 9×6); netherite gear, like the chestplate, survives fire and lava. Diamond gear is crafted from iron gear and eight diamonds, and netherite gear smithed from diamond gear with a netherite upgrade template and a netherite ingot; each keeps every upgrade where it sits, its Level, and the suit's FE. Three more Gear Upgrades: **Shield** (2×2) absorbs about 21% of each hit that armor leaves, spending 200 FE for each point it absorbs, and nothing once the suit is empty; several shields absorb more together and still spend each point at their mean price, not their sum; **Jump** (1×2) raises jump strength by about 13% while the suit has FE, each jump spending 50 FE; **Night Vision** (1×1, one per suit) gives night vision while it draws 10 FE/t, and leaves a potion's alone. A shield Level adds 5% absorbed, a jump Level 5% jump strength and 10 FE a jump; a night vision Level takes about 1 FE/t off its draw, less as it nears the `nightVisionFloor` server config (1 FE/t), which it never goes under. Shield and jump upgrades sum toward the `shieldCeiling` (80% absorbed) and `jumpCeiling` (+50% jump strength, under three blocks) server configs and never reach them. (#3)
- In the Gear Grid, a shift-click sends an upgrade to the inventory when it has room, and a click on an upgrade with the same upgrade at the same Level in hand adds it to that stack. (#3)
- The suits and upgrades have Gearworks' own art. Each tier has an icon of its own, Futureazoo's flight chestplate retinted to its material with an orange gem (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), and is worn in a look of its own, plates of its material around a glowing orange gem, where it used to look like a plain chestplate; the upgrade icons are drawn as one set. (#7)
- The suit's gem glows: worn, it is lit in the dark and pulses every two seconds, and the suits' icons pulse with it.
- **Flight** (2×5, one per suit), the capstone, fits only netherite gear's grid: creative-style flight while the suit has FE, drawing 200 FE/t in the air. Each Level takes about 20 FE/t off the draw, less as it nears the `flightFloor` server config (20 FE/t), which it never goes under, and never changes how fast the player flies. A suit that runs dry mid-air lets the player down slowly, at no cost and without fall damage. It is crafted from four netherite ingots, four feathers and a nether star, and its Levels cost netherite, then diamonds, then nether stars. (#4)
- The Gear Bench has art of its own, a plated casing with an orange core let into its work top, where it used to borrow the smithing table's top and iron block sides, and the suit it holds floats above it, turning, for every player nearby to see.

### Consumers

- An item with a `gearworks:gear_upgrade` item data map entry (`width`, `height`, `stat`, `amount`, optional `draw`) fits the Gear Grid with no code; `gearworks:gear_grid` sizes each suit's grid, at most 9 columns by 6 rows. The suit exposes `Capabilities.Energy.ITEM` over the sum of its batteries. (#1)
- A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry takes optional `amount_per_level` and `draw_per_level`, added once per Level. The `gearworks:level_cost` item data map prices an upgrade's Levels (`energy`, `growth`, and `stages` of up to four `{tag, count}` materials each from a Level `from` on); an upgrade without an entry is never raised. An upgrade's Level is the `gearworks:level` component. The Gear Bench exposes `Capabilities.Item.BLOCK` and `Capabilities.Energy.BLOCK` on every face. (#2)
- A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry's `stat` also takes `shield` (`amount` the fraction of a hit absorbed, `draw` the FE for each point absorbed), `jump` (`amount` the fraction of jump strength added, `draw` the FE a jump spends) and `night_vision` (`draw` FE per tick, which `draw_per_level` lowers toward the `nightVisionFloor` instead of raising; `amount` is not read; a suit holds one). `gearworks:gear_grid` sizes `gearworks:diamond_gear` and `gearworks:netherite_gear`, which expose `Capabilities.Energy.ITEM` as iron gear does. (#3)
- A `gearworks:gear_upgrade` entry's `stat` also takes `flight` (`draw` FE per tick in the air, which `draw_per_level` lowers toward the `flightFloor`; `amount` is not read; a suit holds one). The suit gives flight through NeoForge's `neoforge:creative_flight` attribute, with the modifier `gearworks:gear_flight`, and leaves any other source of flight alone. (#4)
